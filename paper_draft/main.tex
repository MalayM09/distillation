\begin{abstract}
% =============================================================================
Medical foundation models such as MedSAM (91M parameters) cannot run on
handheld ultrasound devices, whose embedded NPUs admit fewer than
3M parameters under INT8 deployment constraints. Knowledge distillation
(KD) is the canonical bridge, yet the regime of \emph{capacity-ratio}
$R = N_T/N_S$ under which it actually transfers accuracy at edge scale
has not been established empirically.
We study this question on the BUSI breast-ultrasound benchmark with a
six-row ablation matrix at three random seeds and paired-bootstrap
inference ($n = 10{,}000$ resamples). Three findings:
\textbf{(i)} The \emph{Capacity Gap} is real. Direct InfoNCE distillation
from MedSAM into a $<$3M EdgeUNet ($R\!\approx\!30$) yields no mean
Dice lift over supervised training ($\Delta\!=\!-0.0005$, $p\!=\!0.733$).
It does, however, reduce seed variance $3.5\times$.
\textbf{(ii)} Capacity-matched CNN$\to$CNN distillation \emph{works
robustly.} A 15M HA-Net teacher distilled into the same EdgeUNet
($R\!\approx\!5$, MSE$+$KL) lifts mean Dice by $+0.0221$
($\mathrm{CI}_{95}\!=\![+0.0203, +0.0233]$, $p\!<\!0.001$).
\textbf{(iii)} Teacher-Assistant KD (TAKD) does not bridge the gap.
The full MedSAM$\to$HA-Net$\to$EdgeUNet cascade does not exceed direct
A2 ($\Delta\!=\!-0.0043$, $p\!=\!0.067$), and a Hop-1 hyperparameter fix
that significantly improves the assistant ($+0.0152$ Dice, $p\!<\!0.001$)
\emph{does not propagate} to the student ($\Delta\!=\!-0.0007$,
$p\!=\!0.808$). We attribute this to a \emph{per-rung} Capacity Gap:
Hop-2's MSE$+$KL transfer has its own student-side absorption ceiling
that is independent of teacher quality. We conclude that hardware
constraints dictate distillation topology, and \emph{CNN$\to$CNN at
$R\!\approx\!5$ is the optimal edge recipe} on this task.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
knowledge distillation, edge AI, medical image segmentation, foundation
models, teacher-assistant knowledge distillation, breast ultrasound
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
% =============================================================================

Handheld breast-ultrasound probes are increasingly deployed in
low-resource settings to screen for lesions that radiologists would
otherwise contour by hand~\cite{busi}. Two constraints limit the
deployable model: (a) the embedded NPUs in the probes (Hexagon, Ethos-U,
CoreML) admit roughly \textbf{3M parameters} under INT8 quantization
with no transformer or unfused attention operators; (b) prompt-free
inference is required, because clinicians cannot reliably annotate
bounding-box prompts during real-time scanning. Both constraints rule
out direct deployment of contemporary medical foundation models. The
state-of-the-art for medical mask generation, MedSAM~\cite{medsam}, is a
91M-parameter ViT-B and requires a user bounding-box prompt.

The canonical bridge is knowledge distillation
(KD)~\cite{hinton2015distilling}: train a small, prompt-free student
under supervision augmented with a signal from a larger teacher. KD's
mathematical formalism does not, however, predict the regime in which
it is empirically beneficial. Prior work in image classification has
informally identified a ``capacity ratio'' $R = N_T / N_S$ above which
distillation gains
collapse~\cite{mirzadeh2020takd,cho2019efficacy}, but the
edge-segmentation regime ($R$ on the order of 10--30, dense pixel
labels, small lesion-to-background ratios) has not been characterized.

We investigate three concrete questions on the BUSI benchmark:
\textbf{(Q1)} Does direct distillation from MedSAM ($R\!\approx\!30$)
lift the mean Dice of a $<$3M edge student? \textbf{(Q2)} Does
capacity-matched CNN$\to$CNN distillation ($R\!\approx\!5$) lift the
same student? \textbf{(Q3)} Does inserting a 15M intermediate assistant
(Teacher-Assistant KD~\cite{mirzadeh2020takd}) bridge the gap when
direct distillation fails?

Our contributions are four:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Empirical validation of the Capacity Gap.} Direct
InfoNCE-based distillation from MedSAM (91M) into a 2.2M EdgeUNet does
not lift mean Dice ($\Delta\!=\!-0.0005$, $p\!=\!0.733$, 3 seeds), but
reduces seed variance by $3.5\times$.
\item \textbf{Capacity-matched distillation as the working regime.} A
15M HA-Net teacher distilled into the same EdgeUNet under MSE$+$KL lifts
mean Dice by $+0.0221$ ($p\!<\!0.001$); the bootstrap CI width is just
$0.003$ Dice, the tightest signal in our matrix.
\item \textbf{Honest negative result on naive TAKD, doubly armored.}
TAKD does not exceed direct distillation. We rule out Hop-1
loss-weight tuning as the explanation: dropping the InfoNCE weight from
$1.0$ to $0.25$ significantly improves the assistant ($+0.0152$ Dice,
$p\!<\!0.001$) but the improvement does not propagate to the student
($\Delta\!=\!-0.0007$, $p\!=\!0.808$).
\item \textbf{A per-rung Capacity Gap interpretation.} We argue that
MSE feature matching acts as a \emph{low-pass capacity filter}: the
student's bottleneck has its own absorption ceiling, and improvements
in teacher quality above that ceiling do not translate into improved
Dice. The cascade therefore does not stack.
\end{enumerate}

Code, per-epoch training histories, and the multi-seed bootstrap
artefacts are released at\\
\url{https://github.com/MalayM09/distillation}.

\section{Related Work}
% =============================================================================

\paragraph{Medical foundation models.} MedSAM~\cite{medsam}, a
ViT-B-based adaptation of SAM~\cite{kirillov2023sam} to medical imagery,
sets the contemporary upper bound for mask generation across more than
30 anatomies. It is, however, a 91M-parameter prompt-conditioned model
and is therefore inapplicable at the edge without distillation. We use
its image-encoder feature map (256-d at 64$\times$64) as a frozen
teacher signal throughout.

\paragraph{Knowledge distillation.} The Hinton response-distillation
formulation~\cite{hinton2015distilling} matches student and teacher
logit distributions under temperature scaling.
FitNets~\cite{romero2014fitnets} extended this to intermediate feature
maps via MSE. Both approaches assume an architectural family match
between teacher and student. Contrastive
distillation~\cite{tian2019crd,chen2020simclr} relaxes this assumption
by aligning embeddings up to a learned projection; we adopt dense
spatial InfoNCE in the ViT$\to$CNN regime, where direct MSE between
disparate coordinate systems is ill-defined.

\paragraph{Teacher-Assistant KD.} Mirzadeh et
al.~\cite{mirzadeh2020takd} observed that the KD gain on CIFAR
classification decays once the teacher exceeds the student capacity by
more than approximately $8\times$, and proposed inserting an
intermediate model to decompose the transfer into two shorter hops.
This thesis motivated the design space we test here. To our knowledge,
the present paper is the first to empirically grade the TAKD claim on
edge-budget ($<$3M) medical segmentation.

\paragraph{Edge medical segmentation.} Several prior works have produced
\emph{deployable} ultrasound segmentation networks under 3M
parameters~\cite{cao2022swinunet}, but to our knowledge none directly
tests \emph{when} distillation from a foundation model is the right
training recipe at this scale.

\paragraph{Teacher (MedSAM, 91M).} We use the public MedSAM ViT-B
checkpoint~\cite{medsam}. To make distillation tractable on a 16\,GB
GPU, we cache the image-encoder output once per BUSI sample to disk as
an FP16 tensor of shape $256\!\times\!64\!\times\!64$; MedSAM is
never loaded during student training.

\paragraph{Assistant (HA-Net, 15.2M).} A custom Hybrid Attention
U-Net~\cite{woo2018cbam}. The encoder is ResNet-18 in layout with
channels $[64, 128, 256, 512]$ across four stride-2 stages; a
$1\!\times\!1\!\to\!3\!\times\!3\!\to\!1\!\times\!1$ residual
refinement block lives at the bottleneck. Skips are gated by CBAM
channel-and-spatial attention. The decoder is bilinear-upsample $+$
concat $+$ two $3\!\times\!3$ Conv-BN-SiLU at each of four stages. The
network exposes a 512-channel bottleneck at stride $/32$ as its
distillation surface. HA-Net is \emph{not} deployable at the edge --- it
exists purely as a distillation intermediate.

\paragraph{Student (EdgeUNet, 2.19M).} A vanilla 6-level U-Net with
channel widths $(16, 32, 64, 96, 144, 192)$, fixed-scale bilinear
upsampling, MaxPool downsampling, and BN-ReLU activations only. No
attention, no LayerNorm, no GroupNorm. The total parameter count is
\textbf{2{,}192{,}833 ($<$3M)} and the architecture admits a static
ONNX-Resize-only export at opset 17 (verified). Its bottleneck is
$(192, 8, 8)$ at stride $/32$, spatially aligned with HA-Net's.

\subsection{Topology-Conditional Distillation Losses}

The choice of distillation objective is fixed by the topological
relationship between teacher and student.

\paragraph{Rule 1 --- ViT$\to$CNN: Dense InfoNCE.} MedSAM emits ViT
patch tokens whose absolute magnitudes, channel ordering, and spatial
layout are not commensurable with a CNN feature map. Direct MSE or KL
across the boundary is ill-posed. We use dense per-spatial-location
InfoNCE~\cite{chen2020simclr}, with a small projection head $g_\phi$
mapping the CNN bottleneck to MedSAM's $(256, 64, 64)$ grid:
\begin{equation}
\Lnce = -\frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{P}}
\log\frac{\exp(\langle g_\phi(f_S^{(i)}),\,z_T^{(i)}\rangle / \tau)}
{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}}\exp(\langle g_\phi(f_S^{(i)}),\,z_T^{(j)}\rangle / \tau)}
\label{eq:nce}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{P}$ is the set of foreground anchors (lesion locations
in the down-sampled mask), $\mathcal{N}$ is the set of background
locations in the \emph{same image} (no cross-batch negatives), features
are L2-normalized along the channel axis, and $\tau = 0.07$.

\paragraph{Rule 2 --- CNN$\to$CNN: MSE feature matching $+$ KL logit
KD.} When teacher and student share an architectural family,
coordinate systems are approximately diagonal-linearly related;
direct alignment is then mathematically optimal:
\begin{align}
\Lmse &= \frac{1}{N}\sum_i \lVert h_\psi(f_S^{(i)}) - f_T^{(i)} \rVert_2^2 \label{eq:mse}\\
\Lkl  &= T^2 \cdot \mathrm{KL}\!\left[\sigma(z_T/T) \,\Vert\, \sigma(z_S/T)\right] \label{eq:kl}
\end{align}
with $h_\psi$ a $1\!\times\!1$ channel-projection from the student's
192 channels to the teacher's 512, and $T = 2.0$ for the logit
distillation.

\paragraph{The TAKD cascade.} Our full pipeline composes the two rules
(Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}):
\[
\text{MedSAM} \xrightarrow[\text{Rule 1}]{\Lnce} \text{HA-Net}
\xrightarrow[\text{Rule 2}]{\Lmse + \Lkl} \text{EdgeUNet}.
\]
Each hop adds the supervised segmentation loss $\Lseg = $ BCE $+$ Soft
Dice on the student logits. The decomposition routes the
high-information CNN-native objectives through the second hop, where
they are mathematically admissible.

\subsection{Numerical Stability and AMP}

Both InfoNCE and KL are computed in FP32 even under AMP autocast: the
contrastive log-sum-exp at $\tau\!=\!0.07$ produces values
($e^{14}\!\approx\!1.2\!\times\!10^6$) above FP16's representable range,
and the KL temperature-scaled softmax suffers similar precision loss.
Feature MSE is also accumulated in FP32. The segmentation
BCE $+$ Dice remains in the autocast dtype.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
% =============================================================================

\paragraph{Dataset.} BUSI~\cite{busi} contains 647 lesion cases across
benign and malignant subsets at native resolutions ranging from
roughly $320$ to $600$ pixels per side. Multi-component lesions
contribute multiple mask files which we OR-merge into a single binary
mask. The dataset's small `normal' subset (no lesion) is excluded; the
edge task is binary lesion segmentation.

\paragraph{Splits and seeds.} We run each ablation under three random
$80/20$ train/val splits indexed by seeds
$\{20260530, 42, 1337\}$. Splits are deterministic given the seed.
Reported variance is across these three independent runs.

\paragraph{Training protocol.} All models are trained for 40 epochs with
AdamW ($\mathrm{lr}\!=\!3\!\times\!10^{-4}$, $\mathrm{wd}\!=\!10^{-4}$),
batch size 16, FP16 mixed precision, and \emph{hflip-only}
augmentation. Rotation augmentation is forbidden because it breaks the
$(i, j)$ student-anchor / MedSAM-teacher spatial correspondence on
which the InfoNCE positive-pair construction depends; we apply the
hflip synchronously to image, mask, and MedSAM feature tensor (its
spatial $W$ axis). Training runs on a single 16\,GB Kaggle T4 GPU.

\paragraph{Resolution-faithful evaluation.} Following recent medical
imaging benchmarking practice, we evaluate Dice and IoU at the
\emph{native} ultrasound resolution rather than at the 256$\times$256
training resolution: predicted logits are bilinearly upsampled to the
original $(H, W)$ of each test image, sigmoid-thresholded at 0.5, and
compared against the original-size GT mask. This protocol is applied
uniformly to every row of the matrix.

\paragraph{Statistical inference.} For each pairwise comparison we
compute the paired-bootstrap mean delta over the three seeds with
$n\!=\!10{,}000$ resamples. We report mean $\pm$ std of best-epoch
Dice across seeds, paired $\Delta$, 95\% bootstrap CI, and two-sided
$p$-value. The decision rule pre-registered in our ablation document
required $p\!<\!0.05$ on $\Delta_{\text{A3}-\text{A2}}$ for the TAKD
claim to hold.

\subsubsection{The Capacity Gap is real (Q1)}

Direct InfoNCE distillation from MedSAM to EdgeUNet ($R\!\approx\!30$)
produces no significant mean Dice lift over supervised training:
$\Delta_{\text{A1}-\text{B1}} = -0.0005$, $p\!=\!0.733$. The per-seed
deltas alternate sign ($-0.0087$, $+0.0230$, $-0.0157$) and average to
within $10^{-4}$ Dice of zero. This empirically validates the
\emph{Capacity Gap} hypothesis: at this ratio, the 3M student lacks the
function-class expressivity to absorb the 91M teacher's representation,
and the InfoNCE objective degenerates from a knowledge transfer to a
regularizer.

\textit{A robust auxiliary finding.} The seed standard deviation of A1
($0.0063$) is roughly $3.5\times$ lower than that of B1 ($0.0222$).
Contrastive distillation thus acts as a strong \emph{variance
regularizer} even when mean Dice is unchanged --- a property that
matters in clinical deployment, where seed-level reproducibility is
itself a desideratum.

\subsubsection{Capacity-matched distillation works (Q2)}

Substituting MedSAM for the 15M supervised HA-Net teacher and switching
to Rule 2 (MSE$+$KL) yields a robust lift over the supervised floor:
$\Delta_{\text{A2}-\text{B1}} = +0.0221$ with 95\% bootstrap CI
$[+0.0203, +0.0233]$ and $p\!<\!0.001$. The per-seed deltas
($+0.0227$, $+0.0203$, $+0.0233$) are remarkably uniform, yielding a
CI width of just 0.003 Dice; this is the tightest statistical signal in
our matrix. A2 also statistically matches its supervised teacher's
ceiling ($\Delta_{\text{A2}-\text{B2}}\!=\!-0.0074$, $p\!=\!0.300$).
We interpret these jointly as the cleanest empirical demonstration
available, on this task, that the load-bearing variable for KD
benefit is \emph{capacity ratio} ($R\!\approx\!5$ admits the transfer;
$R\!\approx\!30$ does not), not ``distillation'' nor
``foundation-model knowledge.''

\subsubsection{Naive TAKD does not bridge the gap (Q3)}

The full MedSAM$\to$HA-Net$\to$EdgeUNet cascade scores Dice $0.7536\pm
0.0230$, which neither significantly exceeds direct A2 nor reaches the
supervised ceiling B2 (Table~\ref{tab:tests}). The
pre-registered TAKD decision rule
$\Delta_{\text{A3}-\text{A2}}\!>\!0$ at $p\!<\!0.05$ fails: $\Delta =
-0.0043$, $p = 0.067$, trending negative if anything. A3 \emph{does}
beat B1 (no-distillation floor) at $p\!<\!0.001$, but provides no
advantage over the simpler A2 setup. The cascade is therefore
strictly more expensive without being strictly better.

\subsection{Hop-1 Hyperparameter Ablation: The Propagation Failure}
\label{sec:hop1-ablation}

Why does TAKD fail? The most plausible explanation is the
\emph{loss-weight imbalance} in Hop 1. With $\lambda_{\text{NCE}} =
1.0$ (the default in our default protocol), the InfoNCE term dominates
$\Lseg$ by a factor of 5--20$\times$ throughout Hop-1 training; the
HA-Net assistant may be over-optimized for MedSAM-token alignment at
the cost of segmentation fidelity. We test this hypothesis directly by
re-running Hop 1 (and the dependent Hop 2) across the same three seeds
with $\lambda_{\text{NCE}} = 0.25$, all other hyperparameters held
constant. The results are reported in Tables~\ref{tab:lambda-ablation}
and~\ref{tab:propagation}.

$+0.0152$ Dice over the $\lambda\!=\!1.0$ baseline, monotonically

This result is far stronger than a single negative TAKD finding: it
rules out the obvious explanation. The cascade does not fail because
$\lambda_{\text{NCE}}$ was mistuned. It fails because the Hop-2 transfer
itself imposes a capacity ceiling that the assistant's quality cannot
penetrate.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Hop-2 as a Low-Pass Capacity Filter}

The propagation failure documented in Section~\ref{sec:hop1-ablation}
is best understood through the per-batch Hop-2 loss statistics. At
$\lambda_{\text{NCE}}=1.0$, the converged feature-MSE plateau is
$\sim$0.59--0.85 across seeds; under the improved-teacher condition
($\lambda_{\text{NCE}}=0.25$), the converged MSE plateau is
$\sim$0.77--0.94 --- equal or \emph{higher}. The KL term shows the
same pattern (mean converged KL: $\sim$0.04 with the old teacher,
$\sim$0.04--0.24 with the new one). A better teacher produces
\emph{harder} targets for the 3M student to mimic, but does not enable
better student segmentation.

This is the central observation of our paper: \textbf{MSE feature
matching has a student-capacity ceiling that is independent of teacher
quality}. Once the teacher's representation exceeds the complexity the
student can fit, further teacher improvements produce gradient signal
the student cannot translate into segmentation accuracy. Concretely,
the student's $1\!\times\!1$ projector $h_\psi$ (192$\to$512 channels)
is a linear map of fixed rank 192; teacher feature directions outside
that rank are simply discarded. Hop-2 thus acts as a
\emph{low-pass capacity filter}: it transmits the components of the
teacher representation that lie within the student's $192$-dimensional
column space and silently throws away the rest.

\subsection{Per-Rung Capacity Gap: Why the Cascade Does Not Stack}

The mechanism that kills direct distillation at $R\!\approx\!30$ also
operates inside the cascade at $R\!\approx\!5$. Each rung of TAKD
suffers \emph{its own} Capacity Gap independently. Improvements above
the rung's capacity ceiling do not propagate downstream. This is not
predicted by the TAKD literature's
function-class-bridging
argument~\cite{mirzadeh2020takd}: shortening the capacity ratio per
hop reduces the gap, it does not eliminate it. We conjecture
(without proving) that the same mechanism would re-emerge if one
inserted yet another assistant between HA-Net and EdgeUNet: the new
rung's $R\!\approx\!2$ transfer would still saturate at the
192-dimensional student bottleneck.

\subsection{Practical Recommendation}

For deployment, A2 dominates A3 on every axis we measure: equal or
better Dice, simpler training pipeline (one teacher rather than two,
no MedSAM dependency at training time after the assistant is trained),
fewer hyperparameters ($\lambda_{\text{MSE}}, \lambda_{\text{KL}}, T$
in Hop 2 only, instead of those plus $\lambda_{\text{NCE}}, \tau$ in
Hop 1), and equal compute. We therefore recommend
\textbf{capacity-matched CNN$\to$CNN distillation at $R\!\approx\!5$}
as the working recipe for edge medical segmentation, and we recommend
\emph{against} naive Teacher-Assistant cascades that route through ViT
teachers.

\subsection{Limitations and Threats to Validity}

(1) The bootstrap inference is over three paired observations per
test; while this is consistent with standard medical-imaging
multi-seed protocols, a five- or seven-seed replication would tighten
all CIs.
(2) Our distillation objectives are limited to InfoNCE (Rule 1) and
MSE$+$KL (Rule 2). Layer-wise feature distillation, attention
transfer~\cite{zagoruyko2017at}, and relational
distillation~\cite{park2019rkd} were not tested; one of these may
recover TAKD by replacing the rank-limited MSE bottleneck.
(3) Our student is fixed at 2.19M parameters with a specific
6-level U-Net layout. A wider student bottleneck (e.g., 256 channels)
could in principle absorb a richer teacher representation, but at the
cost of the INT8 edge budget.
(4) Generalization to other ultrasound datasets (thyroid, fetal) is
not characterized in this work.

% =============================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
% =============================================================================

We empirically grade three claims about foundation-model distillation
for edge medical segmentation. \textbf{(i)} The Capacity Gap at
$R\!\approx\!30$ is real and prevents direct InfoNCE distillation from
lifting mean Dice. \textbf{(ii)} Capacity-matched CNN$\to$CNN
distillation at $R\!\approx\!5$ delivers a robust $+0.022$ Dice lift
with $p\!<\!0.001$. \textbf{(iii)} Naive Teacher-Assistant KD does not
bridge the gap, and the failure is not Hop-1 hyperparameter tuning: a
$\lambda_{\text{NCE}}$ reduction that significantly improves the
assistant produces zero net change at the student. We interpret this
as a \emph{per-rung Capacity Gap} mediated by an MSE-induced low-pass
filter on the Hop-2 transfer. The practical implication is direct:
hardware constraints dictate distillation topology, and CNN$\to$CNN
distillation at $R\!\approx\!5$ is the optimal training recipe at the
$<$3M edge budget on this task. We release the code, the
three-seed bootstrap artefacts, and our per-epoch training histories
to enable reproduction and to invite future work on Hop-2
architectures that may, eventually, rescue the cascade.
